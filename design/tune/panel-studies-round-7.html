What changed, and what is still open

Key

The box shows the root. Its menu lists the seven natural notes; the stacked ♯ and ♭ switches raise or lower the chosen one, and clicking the lit one returns to the natural. E♯, B♯, C♭ and F♭ are greyed out. The parameter is still one of twelve pitch classes; which spelling you chose, A♯ or B♭, is saved as panel state beside the parameters.

Ref A

440 is ISO 16, the international standard. 442 and 443 are what most German, Austrian, Italian and French orchestras tune to, 444 a few go higher still, and 415 is the period-instrument Baroque standard. 432 is the “Verdi tuning” people ask for. Custom accepts any value from 380 to 480 Hz, for a track bounced off-speed. No schema change: Ref A was already continuous.

Menus in the plugin

A JUCE PopupMenu shown against its target component already flips upward and stays on the display the window is on, so the real menus inherit the behaviour this page imitates. They take the suite's LookAndFeel for colour and type.

Two modes

Classic or Hybrid is the banner across the top, and decides what the lower section draws. The banner is wider than the suite's 70 × 26 switch on purpose; it is the one control that reshapes the panel. The lower section is a clock around Retune: Vibrato and Flex at 10 and 2 o'clock in both modes; on Hybrid, Glide and Shift at 8 and 4, and Formant Keep or Follow under Retune, between them. Controls a mode does not use are not drawn, and nothing moves into their places; their parameters stay in the host, so automation written for Hybrid survives a trip through Classic.

Formant: Keep or Follow

Keep leaves the singer's formants where they are, Hybrid's natural sound. Follow lets them move with the correction the way Classic's do, while notes still move Hybrid's way and can glide. Shift offsets them either way. The parameter is the same Formant Correct switch; only the labels changed.

The hide rule, protected

Which controls Classic hides is now one list in the plugin's code, isHybridOnly(), and a test holds it to the sound: each of Glide, Formant and Shift must change nothing at all on Classic and something on Hybrid. If either ever stops being true, the build fails before the panel can hide a working control or show a dead one.

Live and Studio

Independent of the mode. Both engines share one latency contract: Live rests at 0.4 ms and reports 0, Studio reports the same fixed figure for either engine, so switching modes never moves the host's delay compensation.
