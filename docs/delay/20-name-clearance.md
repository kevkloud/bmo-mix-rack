## 3. "Dwell" as a generic control label

Confirmed: "Dwell" is a long-standing generic knob/parameter name on spring reverb circuits (classic Fender-style tank reverbs, Princeton Reverb, etc.) and on some reverb/delay pedals (e.g., EarthQuaker Devices Ghost Echo uses a Dwell control for decay length). This descriptive, industry-standard usage as a *parameter name* is well-established and widespread.

This cuts both ways: it makes it very unlikely anyone could claim exclusive rights over the word "dwell" for a knob label (helps you), but it also means "Dwell" alone is a weak, semi-descriptive mark in audio-effects contexts — harder to enforce and more likely to draw an office action for descriptiveness if filed alone. Pairing it with "BMO" (BMO Dwell) as the brand differentiator is the standard mitigation.

## Overall risk read: LOW–MEDIUM

- No conflicting audio-plugin/pedal/hardware product found under the "Dwell" name.
- No Class 9/15 trademark conflict surfaced in this shallow pass, but the authoritative registries were not directly queryable here.
- "Dwell" is common descriptive/generic vocabulary in this exact product category, which weakens any third party's exclusive claim but also weakens "Dwell" alone as your own mark — "BMO Dwell" is the safer filing/branding unit.
- Recommend a real TESS (Classes 9 & 15) and TMview search, plus a basic common-law/domain/app-store check, before committing to the name commercially.
