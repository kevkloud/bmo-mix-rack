# BMO DEQ — the serial-vs-parallel blind run, 2026-09-11

The run record for the listening pass that `deq-topology-listening.md`
describes. That file is the method and the argument; this one is what was
actually rendered, from what, and what the tool measured while it did it.

The method these two follow is `blind-listening-protocol.md`, which was
written from this pass and Tune's rounds.

**Rendered on AURORA**, 2026-09-11, from `add-bmo-deq`, with
`measure_deq render ... --blind 7` (DSP-only Release build). The DSP is the
shipping one: the tool drives `DspCore`, not a stand-in.

**Where the files are:** `C:\Users\thesp\deq-blind\<source>\`, outside the
repository. They are 32-bit float WAVs named `<case>-A.wav` / `<case>-B.wav`,
with `key.txt` beside them and `ANSWERS.md` at the top of the folder. **The
key stays shut until every line of ANSWERS.md has something on it.** Nobody in
this session has opened one.

## What the tool measured, per source

`serial vs parallel` is the worst difference between the two renders, relative
to the input: the more negative, the more alike they are. `max GR` is how hard
the dynamic bands actually worked **on that material** — a case reading 0.0
never engaged, so its pair is two static filters and says nothing about
dynamics.

## Where the dynamics are actually tested

Five sources drive the dynamic bands hard enough to mean anything: the **mix**
(10.0 / 12.0 dB), the **vocal** (10.0 / 10.1), the **drum loop** (7.5 / 8.7),
the **guitars** (5.9 / 7.2) and, half-heartedly, the **room** (3.8 / 4.7). On
the **808** and the **synth**, cases 7 and 8 are effectively two static
filters — listen if curious, but they answer nothing about dynamics, and
`deq-topology-listening.md` says to note that rather than read a verdict into
it.

## Before you listen

- **Check the level.** The full mix renders **above 0 dBFS in every case**, up
  to +5.0 — pull the monitoring down 6 dB before it and leave it there.
  `stacked-boosts` also hits −0.0 dBFS on the vocal and −4.4 on the drums.
  Same gain across A and B of a pair, always.
- **Play a pair back to back**, not a bank of files. The question is per case.
- **Do `vocal-control` first, on every source.** It is the validity check: the
  two renders differ by −32 to −41 dB relative to input, which should be
  inaudible. If that pair is distinguishable, something other than the
  topology is being heard, and nothing else from that source can be trusted.
- **A and B are shuffled per case**, so preferring "A" twice means nothing.

## What the answer decides

Serial was chosen on the measurements (`spec/topology-options.md`) and every
test that can be written is written: the response is the band curves added in
dB, a low cut still cuts under a boost, static bands do not depend on their
order. What is left is whether the difference matters on real material, and
whether anything about serial is worse.

If serial holds, `DspCore.h`'s `Topology` default stays where it is and spec
C4 is revised in the same commit. **Whether a serial/parallel switch is ever
exposed as a control is a separate question, held open until this pass is
heard** (Frosty, 2026-09-11) — and it is the one thing still keeping DEQ's
panel out of the UI pass.

## Picking this up on the other machine

The work is on the fork; the audio is not. What does not travel, and what to
do about it:

**The renders.** They were made on AURORA at `C:\Users\thesp\deq-blind\`,
outside the repository. Either copy the folder (about 40 MB a source), or
**re-render**: `measure_deq render` is deterministic — same source, same
`--blind 7`, same build gives byte-identical files and the same A/B shuffle —
so only the source wav has to travel.

**The blank answers sheet does travel**:
`testing-notes/deq-blind-answers.md` is the copy in git. Put it beside the
audio as `ANSWERS.md`, fill it in there, and commit the filled copy back over
the one in `testing-notes/` when the pass is done.

```
git worktree add -b deq-work ../bmo-mix-rack-333-deq origin/integration
cmake -S . -B build-dsp -DBMO_DSP_ONLY=ON
cmake --build build-dsp --config Release --target measure_deq
build-dsp/tools/Release/measure_deq.exe render <source.wav> <short-out-folder> --blind 7
```

A listening-only pass needs neither the licensed fonts nor the JUCE submodule:
those are for the panel, not the renderer. **Keep the output path short** —
Windows stops at 260 characters, and the tool refuses a folder it cannot use
rather than write half a set. **Name the machine** in anything written down.

**Two machines, two branches.** While DEQ is worked on here, Tune is on
`bmo-tune-work`. Both merge into `integration`, so fetch before merging and
never rebase a branch the other machine has.

## The result, 2026-09-11 on AURORA

Frosty listened to all 51 pairs across the seven sources. Every answer is in
`deq-blind-answers.md`, with the key decoded beside it. The short of it:

- **All seven controls passed.** Not one was distinguishable, so the chain was
  not leaking a difference and the rest of the answers stand.
- **Every heard difference ran in the direction the measurements predict**,
  without knowing which render was which: serial's stacked cuts read as "much
  more noticeable", its stacked boosts as "louder/harsher", its low cut as
  actually removing what sat under the shelf, and its dynamic cut as "more
  obviously de-essed". Parallel read as "unedited" case after case.
- **Nothing was reported as a fault** on either topology — no pumping, no
  smearing, no clicks, no artefacts. The word "fault" never came up; the
  answers are all about degree.
- **The preferences that went to parallel are all cases where parallel did
  less**: two −12 dB cuts stacking to −24 in serial, two +6 boosts stacking to
  +12, a de-esser catching harder. The preferences that went to serial are the
  cases about a filter doing what its curve says — the 808's fuller low end,
  the guitars' stacked cuts, the vocal's de-esser.

Which is the distinction the decision turns on: **amount is a knob, behaviour
is a topology.** Every parallel preference is reachable in serial by asking
for less; none of the serial behaviours is reachable in parallel at all.

The verdict itself is Frosty's, at the bottom of the answers sheet.

**One method note for the next round:** a single `--blind` seed gives the same
A/B assignment on every source, so a listener who forms an impression of "A"
carries it from source to source. Use a different seed per source. It did not
bite here — the answers track the physics case by case rather than following a
letter — but it could have.
