# The 2026-09-11 shoot-out: BMO Tune RT 0.1 against Antares and Waves

Frosty rendered two songs through three tuners in Ableton and sent the files;
the analysis was done on **AURORA** the same day. The audio is not in the
repository (it may not be ours to publish; the fork is public). On AURORA it
is in the gitignored `field-audio/shootout-2026-09-11/`.

## What was rendered

| | |
|---|---|
| Songs | *Failure* (D major) and *Fuji* (G major), a low male vocal, 44.1 kHz / 16-bit stereo (dual mono) |
| Antares | Auto-Tune Artist, input type Low Male, retune speed 0 / 10 / 20 ms |
| Waves | Waves Tune Real-Time, pitch setting generic, Speed and Note Transition both 0 / 10 / 20 ms |
| BMO | Tune RT 0.1 (`6710e73`), CLASSIC, Live, range Auto, everything else default, Retune 0 / 10 / 20 |
| Each | tuned to the song's key, and an untuned reference |

## What was wrong with the files, and what was done about it

- **Five BMO exports were silent** -- 0 and 10 on both songs and Failure's
  BMO dry: 16-bit dither on digital silence from the first sample, not a
  dropout. Every other track of the same session had audio, and the dry was
  silent too, so an export or routing slip rather than the plugin. Frosty
  re-exported; Failure's redo was all audible, Fuji's `0ms` and `10 ms` were
  the old silent files uploaded again (identical hashes).
- **BMO's Retune was a unitless knob.** 0.1's Retune ran 0-100 and mapped
  exponentially to a time constant: 10 was 1.2 ms, 20 was 3.2 ms; 36.0 and
  47.3 were 10 and 20 ms. Frosty re-exported at 36 and 47.3 as well. This is
  why Retune is now in milliseconds (`retune_ms`, see `modules/tune/params.h`).
- **Failure's BMO redo was chromatic, not D major.** Shown by rendering the
  dry through `bmo-tune-cli` both ways: chromatic matches Frosty's file to
  0.9 cents median pitch, D major does not match at all. Fuji's BMO files are
  G major at the knob values their names say (0.4 c).
- So the BMO figures below are `bmo-tune-cli` renders of the same dry, in key,
  where Ableton's were wrong or missing. The CLI reproduces an Ableton render
  of 0.1 in pitch to 0.3-0.9 c median -- not sample for sample, since the dry
  was a dithered 16-bit export rather than the bits the plugin saw.

## Hard-tune accuracy, in key

Median distance of the output from the nearest in-key note, and the share of
10 ms frames within 5 cents. The dry sits around 35 c, 6-8 % within 5 c.
Measured with `tools/tune/common/Analysis.h`'s ruler over 40 ms frames.

| Failure (D major) | 0 | 10 ms | 20 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antares | **1.2 c / 75 %** | 8.9 / 35 % | 13.1 / 24 % |
| Waves | 3.7 / 59 % | 7.8 / 35 % | 11.2 / 25 % |
| BMO (0.0 / 10 / 20 ms) | 3.3 / 57 % | 10.2 / 33 % | 13.3 / 24 % |

| Fuji (G major) | 0 | 10 ms | 20 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antares | **3.0 / 62 %** | 12.3 / 26 % | 16.4 / 19 % |
| Waves | 4.5 / 52 % | 10.3 / 29 % | 14.5 / 21 % |
| BMO | 4.1 / 54 % | 11.8 / 24 % | 14.7 / 20 % |

At 10 and 20 ms, matched in milliseconds, the three are level. At 0 Antares
is tighter; BMO is level with Waves.

## Where the gap is

The same error, sorted by how fast the dry pitch was moving (cents per 10 ms):

| | 0-2 | 2-5 | 5-10 | 10-20 | 20+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Failure: Antares | 0.2 | 0.6 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 6.6 |
| Failure: Waves | 2.2 | 2.5 | 2.9 | 4.1 | 9.3 |
| Failure: BMO | 0.4 | 1.3 | 2.6 | 5.7 | 14.9 |
| Fuji: Antares | 0.9 | 1.3 | 2.0 | 4.2 | 13.9 |
| Fuji: Waves | 2.4 | 2.4 | 2.5 | 6.0 | 12.7 |
| Fuji: BMO | 1.6 | 2.0 | 2.4 | 5.2 | 22.4 |

On a held note BMO is close to Antares and better than Waves. Moving, it
falls behind in proportion to the speed: its correction lands late. The
reference stimulus measures that lag directly -- about one cycle of the note
-- in `latency-and-lag-2026-09-11.md`, and `tests/dsp/HardTuneTests.cpp`
holds it.

## Everything else measured

| | Antares | Waves | BMO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong-note frames (dry clearly on one note, output on another) | 1.2-2.3 % | 2.7-5.0 % | 1.9-3.6 % |
| Transient events > 10 dB of high-frequency energy over the dry, Failure | 2-5 | 14-17 | 0-1 |
| The same, Fuji (at the same moments for all three: the material) | 27-34 | 47-54 | 26-32 |
| High-frequency energy against the dry | -0.1 to -0.4 dB | -0.5 to -0.6 dB | -0.02 to -0.16 dB |
| Level dips over 6 dB, dropouts | none | none | none |

The large pitch jumps in every file (+1900, +2400 cents) are the ruler
locking onto harmonics, at similar rates for all three, not octave errors in
any tuner. Nothing here looks like a BMO-specific click or dropout.

**Timing in these files is not latency.** Ableton compensated what each
plugin reported (Antares 2.33 ms, Waves and BMO 0), so the offsets in the
exports are only what each plugin did not report: BMO 0.4-1 ms, Antares
2-3 ms, Waves 0.75 ms on Fuji and 10 ms on Failure. True latency is measured
uncompensated in `latency-and-lag-2026-09-11.md`.

## Blind listening, and the Ableton cross-check (later on 2026-09-11)

Frosty listened on AURORA (Apollo Twin X Gen 2, HEDD Type 20 MK2), answers
written before the key was opened (`field-audio/blind-2026-09-11/ANSWERS.md`):

| group | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | what was heard in BMO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Failure 0 ms | Antares | Waves | BMO | pops and clicks, uneven correction, does not settle on notes |
| Failure 10 ms | Antares | Waves | BMO | hunting for pitch, clicks, wobbles throughout |
| Failure 20 ms | Antares | Waves | BMO | notes right, but skipping / dropouts in the pitch hold |
| Fuji 0 ms | Waves | Antares = BMO | | transition steps on faster words (both) |
| Fuji 10 ms | Antares | BMO | Waves | some formant shift |
| Fuji 20 ms | Waves | Antares | BMO | weak at the end of each phrase |

These are the hiccups. They are more than the correction lag: skips and
clicks on held, corrected notes (whole-period splices on a low voice),
hunting, and correction fading at phrase ends. The high-frequency transient
count above missed the skips -- a period repeated or dropped on a low voice
is not a treble burst -- so it is the wrong detector for them. Each needs a
detector that hears it and a failing test before a fix. Not started.

Ableton cross-check (renders in Frosty's `Desktop\BMO TUNE refs`, AURORA,
48 kHz, buffer 2048, Delay Compensation off): Auto-Tune Artist matches our
host render to -105 dB and Waves bit for bit, so `References.h` stands.
Waves' output depends on the host block size (128 and 2048 differ by
-22 dB, worst-case latency 10.62 ms at both; other sizes not yet swept).
BMO in a plain host matches bmo-tune-cli bit for bit at 128 and 2048; in
Ableton it matches until 4.71 s and then differs by -79 dB, which moves no
score. Cause not found -- something in Ableton's playback, not the plugin.
`bmo-tune-hostrender` ran 0.5 s of pre-roll until this; that shifts BMO's
detector grid and changed its output by -28 dB, so pre-roll is now off
unless asked for.

## Round three: the steadier note, heard (2026-09-11, AURORA)

`field-audio/blind-2026-09-11-round3/` on AURORA, made the same way as the
others: the dry and three shuffled letters per group, lined up and
level-matched. Frosty listened on AURORA through a UA Apollo Twin X gen 2
into HEDD Type 20 mk2 monitors, answered every line, then opened the key.
Failure carried round two's BMO (`989a5ef`) against today's (`f2b0f8a`);
Fuji carried 0.1's against today's; Antares was in all three.

| group | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failure 0 ms | Antares: "best, but some audible skips/pops toward the very end" | **BMO now**: "better than C but closer to C than to A -- skips and pops, maybe some kind of overload" | BMO round two: "all kinds of overloading, clicking, popping, skipping" |
| Failure 20 ms | Antares: "clear best, no audible issues" | BMO round two: "fewer skips and clicks than A, but far too many to be usable" | **BMO now**: "worst. Pops and skips at the beginning of every word, and then some more on top" |
| Fuji 0 ms | BMO 0.1: "best" | **BMO now**: "2nd best, a razor-thin margin behind C" | Antares: "worst, only slightly rough transitions" |

**The dwell is not a win yet.** It did what it was asked to do at 0 ms on
Failure -- today's build placed above round two's, the first time a BMO
has been ranked above another BMO on that take by a clear margin -- but at
20 ms it went the other way, and by more: today's build is now the worst
of the three, where round two's was second. The same code, heard at two
speeds, moved in opposite directions.

On Fuji, where BMO was never far behind, today's build and 0.1 are a tie
inside Frosty's own margin ("razor thin"), and both sit above Antares.
That take is not where the work is.

**The lead, in Frosty's words: "pops and skips at the beginning of every
word."** Onsets, not held notes. `CorrectionLaw::tick` only runs the note
decision while `evaluated && voiced`; nothing clears `haveNote`, `note`,
`pendingNote` or `pendingNoteSince` when the voice drops out between
words. Only an empty scale mask clears them. So a word can open on the
previous word's note and, if the new pitch does not clear
`noteClearCents` of margin against it, be held there for `noteDwellMs`
before the switch is allowed. Round two had no dwell, so it switched at
the first evaluation. That is a hypothesis with a mechanism, not a
finding: it is measured next, and no change goes in before a test fails
on it.

## What round three changed, and the wall it found (2026-09-11, AURORA)

The dwell was split from the detector's later checks and each was measured
on its own, same tool, same take, only the DSP differing:

| Failure | on the note | changes | neighbours | jumps | splices 0 ms | splices 20 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| round two `989a5ef` | 90.7 % | 354 | 70 | 59 | 144 | 109 |
| the dwell alone | -- | 294 | 31 | 62 | 147 | 120 |
| the detector's checks alone | -- | 400 | 79 | 60 | 164 | 109 |
| both, as Frosty heard it | 90.2 % | 322 | 33 | 66 | 155 | 122 |

The dwell is what costs the pops: the detector's checks leave the splice
count at round two's at 20 ms. And the splices the dwell adds are not
scattered -- with the field tool's CSV extended to carry them, every one
falls inside a hold:

| Failure, 20 ms | frames aimed off the nearest note | splices in them | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| round two | 116 (0.9 %) | 0 | 109 |
| the dwell alone | 889 (7.2 %) | 14 | 120 |
| both | 945 (7.6 %) | 15 | 122 |

**The mechanism.** A held note is a sustained pitch shift, the engine's
read drifts at that rate, and the window it may drift in is about one
period wide from a rest of 0.40 ms (`ClassicEngine`, `kLiveRest`). Hold
long enough against a singer who has moved -- 91 cents, on this take --
and the read leaves the window and splices a whole period. That splice is
the pop. At retune 0 the correction is over before it drifts; at 20 ms it
is still pulling, which is why Frosty heard it at 20 and not at 0.

**The fix in the tree** (`noteHoldFreeCents`, `noteHoldBudgetCentMs`): a
hold is billed the pull it cannot afford, times how long it carries it,
and is cut when the bill is due. Tests state the law, not a constant.

**The wall.** Frosty's round-two call -- a vibrato that only just crosses
a boundary must hold its note -- *is* a 40 ms hold at up to 60 cents of
pull. It is the same shape as the holds that splice. Every setting swept
that wins the pops back also breaks that vibrato:

| free / budget | splices 20 ms | neighbour flips | the vibrato hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 / 320 (in the tree) | 120 | 41 | holds; 14 of 14 suites pass |
| 50 / 240 | 103 | 42 | fails |
| billed on the margin, 160 | 110 | 38 | fails |

So the knob cannot have both, and Frosty chose (2026-09-11) to keep the
tree green and fix it properly instead: **widen the window** by spending
part of the ~7 ms of latency headroom under the Waves ceiling (10.62 ms;
BMO is at 3.66). A read that may drift further does not splice on a hold,
the dwell survives untouched, and it is the same lever the correction lag
needs. That is the next piece of work, and it is heard before it counts.


## Round four: the wider window, heard (2026-09-11, AURORA)

`field-audio/blind-2026-09-11-round4/`, built from
`blind-manifest-round4.txt`: Antares, the BMO of round three
(`bmo-renders-hold`, the 0.40 ms rest) and BMO with the 4 ms rest
(`bmo-renders-window`, `31b30ef` over `3d365a7`). Frosty listened on
AURORA through a UA Apollo Twin X gen 2 into HEDD Type 20 mk2 monitors,
answered every line, then opened the key. The set aligns by onset, so it
moved the new renders about 3.9 ms: what was ranked is the tuning, not
the added delay.

| group | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failure 0 ms | Antares: "best, no audible issues; some audible transitions" | **BMO now**: "2nd best, some audible pops" | BMO round three: "clear worst" |
| Failure 20 ms | Antares: "best, no audible issues" | **BMO now**: "2nd best, still too many audible pops" | BMO round three: "worst -- definitely the same as the last build" |
| Fuji 0 ms | **BMO now: "perfect"** | Antares: "2nd best" | BMO round three: "worst" |

**The window is the fix.** BMO placed above its own previous build in all
three groups -- at 20 ms it went from last of three to second, and the
build Frosty ranked last is the one he had ranked second in round three,
which is the same judgement twice from opposite directions. On Fuji it
placed above Auto-Tune Artist and was called perfect: the first time any
BMO has beaten a reference on a take.

**What is left is Failure, and it is still splices.** "Still too many
audible pops" at 20 ms, "some audible pops" at 0. The count is down from
120 to 38 at 20 ms and 139 to 44 at 0, so the remaining ones are audible
and worth chasing: the 66 jump flips on scoops (`bmo-tune-field --csv`
now carries splices per evaluation, so they can be located), then the 15
dropouts under 80 ms. Fuji, at 22 splices, is done.

The cost stands at 6.53 ms true latency, level with Antares' 6.49 and
inside the 10.62 ms rule. Frosty has not yet heard it in Ableton, where
the delay is felt rather than aligned away.
